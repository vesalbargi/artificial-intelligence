# Game Theory and Nash Equilibrium

This assignment explores **strategic decision-making in two-player games** using payoff matrices. It focuses on identifying dominated strategies, finding Nash equilibria, and analyzing mixed strategies when players choose actions probabilistically.

## Part 1: Strictly Dominated Strategies and Best Responses

Analyze a game with three strategies per player using **iterated elimination of strictly dominated strategies (IESDS)**.

**Key concepts:**

* Comparing payoffs across strategies.
* Eliminating strictly dominated strategies.
* Identifying best responses in the reduced game.
* Finding all pure-strategy Nash equilibria.

## Part 2: Pure-Strategy Nash Equilibrium

Examine the **Battle of the Sexes** coordination game, where two players have different preferences but benefit from coordinating their choices.

**Key concepts:**

* Identifying pure-strategy Nash equilibria.
* Applying the best-response method.
* Understanding strategic coordination and conflicting preferences.

## Part 3: Mixed Strategies and Expected Payoffs

Analyze a game with three strategies for Player 1 and two for Player 2, including a strategy that is strictly dominated by a mixed strategy.

**Key concepts:**

* Demonstrating strict dominance through a probability mixture of strategies.
* Eliminating a strictly dominated strategy.
* Calculating a mixed-strategy Nash equilibrium using indifference conditions.
* Computing expected payoffs for both players at equilibrium.

## Learning Outcomes

* Interpret and analyze payoff matrices.
* Apply dominance and best-response reasoning.
* Distinguish pure-strategy and mixed-strategy Nash equilibria.
* Use expected payoffs and indifference conditions to solve strategic games.
